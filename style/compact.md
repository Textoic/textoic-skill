# House style for prose

These apply to prose you write for a person to read: chat answers, summaries,
commit messages, documentation, design notes. They do not apply to code,
identifiers, file paths, or quoted material.

Start with the shape, because it gives a machine away faster than any word
does. No bold phrase at the head of a paragraph announcing its topic, so open
with the point itself. Do not give every idea the same amount of room, because a big
idea earns three paragraphs and a small one earns a clause. Do not close by
restating what you already said, and vary how paragraphs are built rather than
repeating "state the point, elaborate, example, wrap up".

Then the sentences. Use the active voice, one idea per sentence, and name the
actor before you give them the verb. Say what a thing is rather than what it is
not, so "not just X but Y", "not A, but B" and "A, not B" all become the plain
claim. Drop trailing gerund clauses: "..., enabling teams to scale" becomes a
clause with a subject in it. Cut bursts of three, like "fast, reliable, and
scalable", down to the one that matters, and cut adjectives that trail a noun
behind a dash or a parenthesis. Vary sentence length, and follow a long
sentence with a short one.

Then the words. Prefer short common words, verbs over nouns, and nouns over
adjectives. Undo nominalizations, so "initialization" becomes "start" and "the
calibration of the model" becomes "we calibrate the model". Drop hedges,
because "may", "might", "could", "seems" and "appears" weaken a claim you
should either make or drop. Skip similes and metaphors and break the idea down
instead. Use "they" for a generic person, never "he/she". Avoid em dashes
between two words, since a comma, a colon or a full stop is almost always what
you meant.

Then the reader, who has not seen what you saw. Explain a name the first time
you use it: a term you coined, a label, a file or a setting gets a few plain
words that say what it is. When you propose or report a method, say first what
it needs to work and where it cannot run, such as one provider, a local model
or a paid service. Give the results after that. Say what you did, what you
checked and what you left undone.

Substance beats every rule above. When following one would cost you accuracy or
clarity, break it.
